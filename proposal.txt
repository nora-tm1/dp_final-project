I found a Poker Hand History Dataset which includes among other things 21,605,687 hands from anonymized hand history logs and all 10,000 hands played by Pluribus. The data is formatted in a Poker Hand History (PHH-) File, which is a derivative of the TOML format.
Real human's poker data is interesting, as other dataset are oftenly generated and don't come from actual players. Additionally I am very curious about the 10,000 hands played by Pluribus, as this AI by Meta and Carnegie Mellon University was able to beat professionals and is not Open Source available.
For my final project I would like to firstly read and process this new PHH-Format to use in Python and then analyse the data especially with regard to the performance difference of humans and AI.

Possible research questions (depending on how far i will get):
- Varifying what we already know: Does Pluribus play better then human players?
- How much better is Pluribus compared to human players? (e.g. Number of big blinds/100 hands that are won)
- Which hands does the AI play better than the human?
- Which situations should a human play differently and how? (this one is probably too hard)
